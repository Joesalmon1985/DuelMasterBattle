DUEL MASTER BATTLE
NODE MAP VISUAL CATALOGUE
=========================

Purpose
-------
A reusable catalogue for visually constructing playable world-node maps.

These are not 56 handcrafted settlements.

The system combines:
- three adjacent terrain/resource hexes;
- their production numbers;
- incident board edges;
- settlement/city state;
- faction identity;
- infection;
- dungeon origin;
- deterministic visual variation.

The result should make world simulation visible through ordinary exploration.


DESIGN RULES
============

- One board node is one playable overworld area.
- Roads/edges are drawn inside nodes, never used as separate playable edge maps.
- Wilderness and developed nodes use the same underlying node identity.
- Settlement growth should preserve recognizable geography.
- Terrain determines primary industry.
- Production number determines industry quantity.
- Resource pairings determine processing buildings.
- Economic scale influences housing.
- Civic buildings create quest locations and narrative demand.
- Initial settlements have tower dungeons.
- Later-founded settlements have cave dungeons.
- Infection changes presentation without replacing the map.
- NPC work loops are cosmetic expressions of the world economy.
- Generated maps must remain deterministic and traversable.


THE THREE TERRAIN-COMBINATION FAMILIES
======================================

With six possible adjacent terrain types and three adjacent hexes there are 56 unordered terrain signatures.

They fall into only three structural families.

A. HOMOGENEOUS: A + A + A
--------------------------
6 combinations.

Examples:
- Wood + Wood + Wood
- Grain + Grain + Grain
- Ore + Ore + Ore

Visual character:
- strong specialization;
- repeated primary industry;
- repeated same-pair processing;
- very clear economic identity.

B. DOMINANT: A + A + B
----------------------
30 combinations.

Visual character:
- one dominant resource district;
- one secondary resource district;
- one same-resource processor;
- two mixed processors.

Example:
Wood + Wood + Ore
- strong logging presence;
- some mining;
- Sawmill;
- Forge x2.

C. MIXED: A + B + C
-------------------
20 combinations.

Visual character:
- balanced economy;
- three different primary industries;
- three different processors;
- visually diverse settlement.

Example:
Wood + Grain + Ore
- logging;
- farming;
- mining;
- Mill;
- Forge;
- Distillery.


NODE DEVELOPMENT STATES
=======================

TYPE N0 — WILDERNESS NODE
-------------------------
No settlement.

Contains:
- tracks/roads for each board edge;
- terrain blend;
- encounter space;
- local environmental props;
- passing units/carts;
- infection overlay;
- signs or landmarks where useful.

Does NOT contain:
- normal housing;
- settlement processing economy;
- civic centre;
- settlement dungeon.

Purpose:
- contrast;
- travel;
- encounters;
- evidence of world geography;
- future development space.


TYPE N1 — NEW/SMALL SETTLEMENT
------------------------------
Contains:
- all production buildings implied by adjacent hexes;
- three processors;
- housing target;
- storehouse;
- central circulation space;
- limited civic/quest buildings;
- roads/tracks;
- local faction/ruler presence as appropriate;
- tower or cave dungeon depending on origin.

Visual character:
- open;
- rural;
- yards between structures;
- industries visually dominate over housing.


TYPE N2 — CITY
--------------
Same place after upgrade.

Contains:
- upgraded production buildings;
- upgraded processing buildings;
- upgraded storehouse;
- denser/upgraded housing;
- stronger civic core;
- market/inn/institutional activity;
- improved roads;
- greater ambient population.

Visual character:
- denser;
- busier;
- more built-up;
- stronger central street/square;
- visibly wealthier or more organised.


TYPE D1 — STARTING TOWER DUNGEON ENTRANCE
========================================

Exists on every settlement created during world setup.

Node-map expression:
- visible tower landmark;
- entrance entity/door;
- exterior props;
- clue/signage if needed;
- dungeon NPCs or guards where appropriate.

Suggested terrain skins
-----------------------
Wood:
- ivy/wooden gallery;
- timber palisade around old stone.

Brick/Hills:
- red-stone or clay-brick tower;
- terraced foundations.

Wool/Pasture:
- hill tower;
- sheep paths around base.

Grain/Fields:
- isolated watchtower rising above fields;
- granary-like outbuildings.

Ore/Mountains:
- stone keep;
- buttressed tower;
- metalwork details.

Wastes:
- weathered ancient tower;
- partially ruined but functional entrance.

The tower's dungeon mechanics are independent of its exterior skin.


TYPE D2 — FOUNDED-SETTLEMENT CAVE ENTRANCE
==========================================

Exists on every settlement founded after initial world creation.

Terrain presentation
--------------------
Wood:
- root-covered cleft;
- cave behind felled timber or old quarry path.

Brick/Hills:
- clay bank;
- quarry cut;
- collapsed kiln tunnel.

Wool/Pasture:
- grassy barrow;
- hillside opening.

Grain/Fields:
- sinkhole;
- buried cellar/old mound exposed during farming.

Ore/Mountains:
- mine-like rock opening;
- natural fissure.

Wastes:
- cracked earth;
- ruin shaft;
- broken subterranean entrance.

The cave should remain visually recognizable as the settlement's dungeon entrance.


ROAD / TRACK ARCHETYPES
=======================

R0 — NO OWNED ROAD
------------------
Render:
- rough track/path to neighboring node.

R1 — ONE ROAD SPUR
------------------
One incident edge is an owned road.

Layout:
- road enters node;
- terminates/converges at settlement centre or main junction;
- other incident edges remain tracks.

R2A — TWO-ROAD THROUGH ROUTE
----------------------------
Two road edges form the primary travel line.

Layout:
- main street/road passes through node;
- third edge branches as track.

R2B — TWO-ROAD BEND
-------------------
Two roads meet at a corner/junction.

Layout:
- settlement develops around bend;
- useful for market/civic square.

R3 — THREE-ROAD JUNCTION
------------------------
All incident edges are roads.

Layout:
- central crossroads/square;
- strongest candidate for:
  - market;
  - inn;
  - storehouse;
  - civic building.

CITY ROAD UPGRADE
-----------------
Settlement roads become:
- wider;
- more regular;
- paved/boarded/engineered where art allows;
- lined with buildings;
- optionally gated.

PRODUCTION COUNT RULE
=====================

For each productive adjacent hex:

6 / 8  -> 5 production structures
5 / 9  -> 4
4 / 10 -> 3
3 / 11 -> 2
2 / 12 -> 1
Wastes -> 0

The count represents visual/economic scale.

Structures may be:
- full buildings;
- attached sheds;
- yards;
- pens;
- plots;
- shafts;
- kilns;

depending on the industry.

Do not require every counted unit to use a large footprint.

SETTLEMENT COMPOSITION ORDER
============================

Recommended generation order:

1. Establish exits for all incident board edges.
2. Draw road/track routes into the node.
3. Establish central circulation/junction.
4. Blend adjacent terrain.
5. Place tower/cave entrance if settlement exists.
6. Place primary production districts.
7. Place three processors.
8. Place central storehouse.
9. Place housing.
10. Place civic/quest buildings.
11. Add scenery/props.
12. Add NPC anchors.
13. Add units/carts/ruler.
14. Apply infection overlay.
15. Validate traversability.


WILDERNESS COMPOSITION ORDER
============================

1. exits;
2. tracks/roads;
3. central or crossing path;
4. terrain blend;
5. terrain landmark;
6. encounter space;
7. scenery;
8. carts/units if present;
9. infection overlay;
10. traversal validation.

AMBIENT WORK-LOOP GRAMMAR
=========================

Every worker loop should be constructed from:

START ANCHOR
-> TRAVEL
-> WORK ANIMATION
-> LOAD PROP
-> TRAVEL
-> DELIVERY ANCHOR
-> UNLOAD
-> OPTIONAL IDLE/SOCIAL
-> RETURN

Examples
--------

WOODCUTTER
tree/logging anchor
-> chop
-> pick up log
-> walk to sawmill/log pile
-> unload
-> return


SAWMILL WORKER
log pile
-> saw
-> pick up plank bundle
-> walk to storehouse
-> unload
-> return


FARMER
field
-> harvest
-> pick up sack
-> walk to mill/storehouse
-> unload
-> return


MILLER
grain delivery
-> operate mill
-> collect flour sack
-> walk to bakery/storehouse
-> unload


MINER
mine entrance
-> disappear/work
-> emerge with ore
-> walk to forge/smelter/storehouse
-> unload


BLACKSMITH
forge
-> hammer
-> cool
-> pick up tools
-> walk to storehouse/market/barracks
-> unload


WEAVER
wool store
-> weave
-> pick up cloth bundle
-> walk to market/storehouse/civic consumer
-> unload


BREWER
grain store
-> stir/tend vat
-> barrel
-> move barrel
-> inn/storehouse


CHARCOAL BURNER
wood pile
-> stack/tend kiln
-> bag charcoal
-> temple/inn/bathhouse/storehouse

INFECTION PRESENTATION STATES
=============================

I0 — CALM
---------
Normal work loops.
Normal traffic.
Open civic buildings.


I1 — UNEASY
------------
Some:
- guards;
- nervous idle;
- inspection;
- barricade preparation.

Most economic loops continue.


I2 — PRODUCTION SUPPRESSED
--------------------------
Normal production visibly stalls.

Workers:
- idle;
- shelter;
- argue;
- guard;
- carry emergency supplies.

Industries:
- doors shut;
- furnaces cold;
- abandoned carts.

Civic:
- healer/watch/temple become busier.


I3 — OVERRUN
------------
Demons visibly dominate.

Use:
- empty streets;
- barricades;
- damaged props;
- fires;
- hidden civilians;
- stopped carts;
- active encounters.

Keep core map identity recognizable so recovery feels meaningful.


CITY-UPGRADE VISUAL RULES
=========================

Do not redraw from scratch.

Upgrade the existing settlement in place.

Production
----------
Improve building tiers and yards.

Processing
----------
Replace with larger/more sophisticated variants.

Housing
-------
- add;
- enlarge;
- densify.

Roads
-----
- improve surface;
- widen key routes;
- create stronger main street/square.

Storage
-------
- enlarge warehouse/granary.

Civic
-----
- add/upgrade market;
- ruler/civic hall;
- inn;
- watch;
- other deterministic institutions.

Population
----------
Increase ambient NPC count.

Props
-----
More:
- carts;
- signs;
- stalls;
- stacked goods;
- lamps;
- civic decoration.

Dungeon
-------
Tower/cave identity remains unchanged.


MAP LEGIBILITY RULES
====================

The player should be able to visually infer:

1. Where can I leave?
2. Which exits are proper roads?
3. What does this place produce?
4. Is this a settlement or city?
5. Where is the centre?
6. Where is storage/market activity?
7. Where are important NPCs likely to be?
8. Where is the tower/cave?
9. Is the place healthy or overrun?

Do not sacrifice navigation clarity for procedural clutter.


PLACEMENT SAFETY RULES
======================

Never generate:
- building on required exit tile;
- prop blocking one-tile mandatory corridor;
- NPC anchor inside collision;
- road disconnected from exit;
- dungeon entrance inaccessible;
- civic quest NPC trapped behind cosmetic geometry.

Always validate:
- all exits reachable;
- dungeon reachable;
- core civic area reachable;
- required quest buildings reachable;
- combat spaces have enough room.


VISUAL VARIATION WITHOUT NEW GAMEPLAY
=====================================

Safe deterministic variation:
- roof shape;
- wall material;
- sign placement;
- yard orientation;
- prop clusters;
- tree/rock distribution;
- fence type;
- building facing;
- crop/pen shape;
- chimney position;
- banner/flag placement;
- road wear;
- small plaza shape.

Avoid variation that changes:
- gameplay reachability;
- authoritative simulation;
- quest logic;
- puzzle state.


DESIGN TARGET
=============

The generator should not attempt to create a unique handcrafted map for every one of the 56 terrain signatures.

Instead:

TERRAIN COMBINATION
+ PRODUCTIVITY
+ ROADS
+ DEVELOPMENT LEVEL
+ FACTION
+ INFECTION
+ DUNGEON ORIGIN
+ DETERMINISTIC LAYOUT VARIATION
=
A DISTINCT, READABLE NODE MAP

A player revisiting the world after many turns should be able to see that it has changed:
- empty crossings become settlements;
- settlements become cities;
- industries reflect local resources;
- productive sites look larger;
- roads appear and improve;
- towers and caves provide exploration;
- carts and workers make trade visible;
- infection disrupts ordinary life;
- recovery brings that life back.
