# FIRST ADVENTURE — THE TRIAL

## Core premise

John is not a chosen hero.

He is not hunting a wizard.

His home has not been destroyed.

He simply lives in **Ashwell**, a small village situated a short walk from a famous magical trial held inside an ancient fortress in the hills.

Once a year, people come from across the country to attempt it.

Most people in Ashwell regard Trial day as a mixture of festival, business opportunity and public insanity.

John has never seriously considered entering.

Until two wizards arrive.

---

# 1. START IN ASHWELL

The game begins with John outside his house in Ashwell.

He has:

- no magic;
- no staff;
- no weave;
- no destiny;
- an ordinary life.

The village is unusually busy.

Travellers are passing through.

Stalls have appeared.

Children are pretending to be wizards.

The inn is full.

Everyone is talking about **the Trial**.

The player can immediately wander around and speak to people.

This establishes something important:

**Ashwell is John's home before it becomes the player's quest hub.**

NPCs know John.

For example:

**Mara**
> You're not thinking of going up there, are you?

**John's neighbour**
> Trial day. Best day of the year for selling beer. Worst day for finding anybody sensible to drink it with.

**Pip**
> Six wizards came through already. One had a bird made of fire.

The player learns naturally that the Trial begins later that day at an ancient complex outside the village.

There is an enormous prize.

Anyone may enter.

Nobody is required to.

---

# 2. THE TWO WIZARDS ARRIVE

After John has walked around the village for a short period, shouting is heard near the road.

Two travelling wizards have arrived.

They are the existing:

- Blue Wizard / Halvard;
- Red Wizard.

Both intend to enter the Trial.

They know each other.

They hate each other.

Their argument becomes a duel.

This should be the player's first sight of high-level wizard combat.

John watches from the edge of the village while two experienced combatants throw four-slot weaves at one another.

The player does NOT understand the mechanics yet.

They simply see how dangerous wizard duelling can be.

Halvard loses.

But unlike the existing opening, **Ashwell does not burn down**.

The fight is violent and spectacular, but localised.

The Red Wizard takes whatever victory means to him and continues toward the Trial.

Before leaving he might look at John and say:

> "Don't stare, villager. You'll see worse before sunset."

He leaves.

This establishes him as a recurring rival rather than the first chapter's final boss.

---

# 3. HALVARD'S STAFF

Halvard is badly wounded.

John approaches.

Halvard explains that the staff carries his magic and his right to enter the Trial.

He gives it to John.

Not because John is secretly special.

Because Halvard is dying and John happens to be there.

Halvard might say:

> "Take it."

John takes the staff.

> "Water first. It always answered me easiest."

The staff reacts.

John learns **WATER**.

His weave becomes **one slot**.

Then Halvard tells him the important part:

> "The seal on the staff is mine. Take it to the Trial gate and they'll recognise it."
>
> "Enter if you want."
>
> "Don't enter because a dying fool told you to."

Halvard dies.

Now the game has given the player power.

It has **not** given the player an obligation.

---

# 4. THE VILLAGE REACTS

John can now wander Ashwell carrying a wizard's staff.

NPC dialogue changes.

Some encourage him.

Some think entering would be suicidal.

Some become fascinated by the staff.

Ashby the Hedge Wizard recognises Halvard's magic.

He becomes the player's first mentor.

Ashby can provide a safe introduction to the combat system.

For example:

> "You have one word and one knot. That's not wizardry yet."
>
> "But it's enough for me to show you what a Ward is."

This becomes the existing **one-slot tutorial battle**.

Rather than fighting a random Flame Wisp because the forest caught fire, John learns the duel system deliberately.

This is much cleaner mechanically.

---

# 5. THE ROAD TO THE TRIAL

John can leave Ashwell and walk toward the Trial grounds whenever the player wants.

The road is full of:

- spectators;
- merchants;
- failed former contestants;
- prospective entrants;
- bookmakers;
- healers;
- other wizards;
- people selling useless "protective" charms;
- families treating the whole event as entertainment.

The closer John gets, the more impressive and disturbing the Trial becomes.

Eventually the player reaches the entrance.

Other competitors are waiting there.

The Red Wizard is amongst them.

He notices Halvard's staff.

He now recognises John.

But he does not immediately fight him.

That rivalry should survive into the dungeon.

---

# 6. THE IMPORTANT CHOICE

At the entrance, a Trial official examines the staff.

Halvard was registered.

His place is now vacant.

Because John carries Halvard's staff and seal, John may take that place.

The official asks:

> "Do you enter the Trial?"

The player receives:

**ENTER THE TRIAL**

**NOT YET**

This is a genuine choice.

Selecting **NOT YET** returns control to the player.

Nothing bad happens.

John can:

- return to Ashwell;
- talk to people;
- practise;
- explore;
- undertake optional encounters;
- return to the gate later.

The Trial remains available.

Selecting **ENTER THE TRIAL** sets:

`entered_trial = true`

and begins the main adventure.

---

# 7. INSIDE THE TRIAL

The existing progression system can now become part of the dungeon.

John enters knowing Water and having a one-slot weave.

The Trial progressively exposes him to additional magic:

### Trial section 1
One-slot opponent.

Teaches the basic Ward/deduction system.

### Trial section 2
John gains FIRE.

Two-slot battles begin.

### Trial section 3
John gains STONE.

Three-slot deduction begins.

### Trial section 4
John gains VINE.

John finally possesses a complete four-slot weave.

From this point onward the game is playing the full Duel Master Battle rules.

This means the current excellent mechanical progression:

**1 → 2 → 3 → 4 slots**

is preserved.

Only its fictional justification changes.

---

# 8. OTHER CONTESTANTS

This is where the Deathtrap-Dungeon-style structure becomes particularly useful.

The Red Wizard should not simply wait at the end like a boss.

He is another contestant.

So are several other wizards.

The player can:

- encounter them repeatedly;
- see evidence of where they have been;
- race them;
- fight them;
- avoid them;
- potentially help them;
- discover some dead;
- watch some become stronger.

This makes wizard duels part of a larger adventure rather than a sequence of unrelated monsters.

The dungeon becomes a tournament in which the competitors are gradually being filtered out.

---

# 9. RED WIZARD ARC

Do NOT make defeating the Red Wizard the objective of the first chapter anymore.

He becomes much more valuable alive.

Initially he is simply:

**the wizard who killed Halvard.**

Then:

**another contestant.**

Then potentially:

**John's principal rival.**

He should occasionally appear ahead of John, behind John or on another path.

Sometimes the player sees what he has done rather than seeing him directly.

Eventually fighting him matters because the player has developed a relationship with him.

The current four-slot Red Wizard battle can therefore remain in the game but move substantially later.

---

# 10. END OF THE NEW OPENING LEVEL

The first major narrative section should end when John voluntarily crosses the Trial threshold.

Something like:

The enormous doors begin to close behind the contestants.

For the first time since leaving Ashwell, turning around is no longer an option.

Ahead, somewhere in the dark, somebody screams.

The Red Wizard smiles.

And walks on.

**THE TRIAL BEGINS.**
